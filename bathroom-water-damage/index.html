Bathroom Water Damage in Port Arthur

Tile, tubs and fixtures are made to get wet on top, but what lies beneath them is not. Every flush and every shower lets a small amount of water by after the caulk cracks, the grout wears down or the seal at the toilet fails. That water gathers where you cannot see it, in the subfloor and inside the tiled wall. In Port Arthur, we locate the entry point, measure how far the water has spread, then dry or take out the wet material.

Slow leaks are the norm here, which means what you find has typically been there longer, and gone further, than it seems. Under a toilet, the subfloor may be soft from top to bottom even though nothing looks wrong with the tile. We use moisture meters and thermal imaging to map the wet area without tearing out more than we need to, then open only the sections that cannot be dried in place.

Signs to Look For

  • The ceiling beneath the bathroom shows a brownish ring or has started to droop
  • Caulk along the tub or shower that is cracked, peeling, or black with mold
  • Dark stains at the base of the toilet, or a bowl that wobbles a little
  • A musty smell that comes back no matter how often the room is cleaned
  • The bottom panel inside the vanity has swelled, stained or bowed
  • Soft, springy flooring alongside the tub or where you stand at the vanity
Behind the Surface

What Hides Behind It

Mold in the Tiled Wall

Neither grout nor tile is a waterproof barrier without help. Mold takes hold on the wall board once that hidden layer remains wet, and no scrubbing can get to it.

Slow Vanity Drips

Supply valves and drain fittings under the sink can drip for months behind stored bottles and towels. The cabinet floor swells, and the water runs through to the subfloor and the wall behind it.

Leaking Shower Pan

With a crack in the liner or pan, water escapes below the shower floor at each use. Up top everything looks fine, yet the framing underneath never dries.

Rot Under the Toilet

When the wax ring gives out, a bit of water escapes every time the toilet is flushed. The water is category 3, and it rots the subfloor around the flange until the toilet starts to rock.

If Nothing Is Done

The Damage Timeline

First days

Water Slips Through

With every shower, the bad caulk or grout lets a small amount of water through. It soaks into the backer board and the edge of the subfloor, with nothing visible yet.

The first several weeks

Subfloor Stays Wet

Showers come too often for the wood beneath the tile to ever dry. Within the wall cavity, and across the bottom of the floor, mold begins.

One to three months

Signs Show Up

The caulk line goes black, the grout gets darker, and tiles work loose. If there is a room below, a stain appears on its ceiling and slowly widens.

Six months and beyond

Structure Weakens

Around the tub or toilet the subfloor goes soft, and the joists may start rotting. Drying alone no longer fixes it, because carpentry and new tile are now part of the repair.

What We Do

How We Handle Bathroom Water Damage

1

Find the Leak

One by one we check the supply lines, toilet, shower and tub, then follow the water under the tile and into the walls with meters and a thermal camera.

2

Remove What Is Ruined

Loose tile, rotted subfloor and soft wall board come out. Where the toilet leaked, we treat the area as contaminated and remove porous material that cannot be cleaned.

3

Dry the Hidden Spaces

Using dehumidifiers and air movers, we dry the wall framing, the subfloor and the ceiling cavity underneath. We apply antimicrobial treatment and confirm dry readings before anything is closed up.

4

Rebuild and Seal

After replacing backer board and subfloor, we can arrange tile work, a new seal at the toilet, fresh caulking and ceiling repair, which closes the leak path for real.
